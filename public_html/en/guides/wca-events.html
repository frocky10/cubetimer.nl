All 17 WCA events explained

Updated 20 October 2026 · 4 min read

The World Cube Association (WCA) has seventeen official events. A competition usually has somewhere between six and ten of them on the schedule, and the 3x3 is almost always one. Here they all are, with how they work and how the results are decided. You can time every one of them in the CubeTimer timer, with official scrambles.

First: how does a round count?

  • Ao5: five attempts, the fastest and slowest are dropped, the average of the other three counts. That's how most events work.
  • Mo3: three attempts, the plain average. For events where an attempt takes a long time.
  • Best of: one, two or three attempts, and only the best one counts. Mainly for blindfolded events.

Exactly how those averages work, including with a +2 or DNF, is in Ao5, Ao12 and your stats.

The cubes: 2x2 up to 7x7

EventFormatWhat to know
3x3x3Ao5The main event. Almost every competition has it, often with three or four rounds.
2x2x2Ao5Short and explosive: at the top a solve takes one or two seconds, and inspection matters as much as the solve itself.
4x4x4Ao5First build the centres and pair the edges, then solve it like a 3x3. With the famous parity: cases that can't exist on a 3x3.
5x5x5Ao5Same approach as the 4x4. Thanks to the fixed centres you get no parity in the last step, but a tricky case can show up when pairing the last two edges.
6x6x6Mo3An attempt easily takes a few minutes. Often with a cumulative time limit for all attempts together.
7x7x7Mo3The biggest official cube. Mostly a matter of stamina and concentration.

The 3x3 in other ways

EventFormatWhat to know
3x3x3 One-HandedAo5With one hand. Most people use their left hand and turn mostly R, U and F, because those are easiest one-handed.
3x3x3 Blindfoldedbest of 3You look at the cube, memorise its state, put on a blindfold and solve it. The time runs from the moment you see the cube, so memorising counts.
3x3x3 Fewest MovesMo3 or best ofNo speed involved: you get an hour, pen, paper and a few cubes, and write down a solution with as few moves as possible. Under thirty moves is already very good.
3x3x3 Multi-Blindbest ofSolve as many cubes blindfolded as you dare, in one attempt of at most an hour (ten minutes per cube). The score is the number of cubes solved minus the number unsolved; on equal scores the fastest wins.
4x4x4 Blindfoldedbest ofThe same as 3x3 blindfolded, but with many more pieces to memorise.
5x5x5 Blindfoldedbest ofThe hardest single-puzzle blindfolded event. An attempt often takes a quarter of an hour or more.

Blindfolded events have no separate inspection: looking and memorising are part of your time.

The other puzzles

EventFormatWhat to know
MegaminxAo5Twelve five-sided faces. If you can do the 3x3, you'll get the megaminx quickly: the same ideas, just more steps.
PyraminxAo5A pyramid with four corners. One of the fastest events; the best solvers take a few seconds.
SkewbAo5Looks like a cube, but turns around its corners. Few moves per solve, which makes it very fast too.
Square-1Ao5Changes shape as you turn it. First you bring it back to a cube shape, then you solve it.
ClockAo5Not a cube but a disc with nine clocks on each side and four pins. You set every clock to twelve o'clock by turning wheels.

Unofficial events in CubeTimer

Besides the seventeen official events, CubeTimer also lets you time the FTO (an octahedron that turns around its faces) and the Master Tetraminx. They don't appear at WCA competitions, but they're popular enough to have their own scrambles.

There used to be more official events. The Magic and the Master Magic were dropped in 2012, and solving the 3x3 with your feet in 2020. Old records for those events are still on the WCA website.

Which event suits you?

Start with the 3x3; there's the most material about it, and what you learn helps with almost every other event. If you want to try something else, the 2x2, Pyraminx and Skewb make good second events: they're cheap, you learn them in an afternoon and you improve quickly. The big cubes and blindfolded take more patience, but the competition at events is smaller there too.
