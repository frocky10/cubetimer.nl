How to choose and set up a speedcube

Updated 20 October 2026 · 5 min read

A speedcube turns lighter, faster and more stably than the cube from a toy shop. There's a huge choice and the model names change every year, so this guide names few models. It does explain what to look for, what the differences between cheap and expensive are, and how to set a cube up.

Why a speedcube?

An ordinary cube locks up easily: turn one layer while another isn't quite straight and it jams. A speedcube is built to cope with that. It cuts corners (corner cutting), so a move still works when the neighbouring layer is a little off. That lets you keep turning smoothly instead of constantly correcting. Even for a beginner it's the difference between a frustrating solve and a fun one.

Magnets: standard nowadays

Almost every speedcube has magnets in its pieces. They pull a layer into the right position as soon as it gets close. That makes your turning more precise and the cube feel more stable. Buying a cube without magnets only makes sense for a very cheap first cube.

On more expensive models you can often adjust the magnet strength. Strong magnets feel stable but heavy, weak ones feel light but sometimes a bit sloppy. There's no right or wrong; it's a matter of feel.

Cheap or expensive?

KindPrice (roughly)What you get
Budget5 to 15 eurosMagnetic, fine for learning and for getting well under twenty seconds.
Mid-range15 to 35 eurosBetter finish, often adjustable, the flagship models of a year or two ago.
Flagship40 to 80 eurosEverything adjustable, often extras such as magnets in the core or maglev (magnets instead of springs), the brand's latest.

The honest story: a budget cube from a known brand is good enough up to a level most people never reach. Some top cubers win competitions on a cube that costs around twenty euros. The difference is in feel, finish and how much you can adjust, not in a few seconds per solve.

Brands

Well-known speedcube brands include GAN, MoYu, QiYi (with its premium line X-Man), YJ and DaYan. They all make both budget and flagship models. Which model is "best" changes every year and differs from person to person. Read and watch a few recent reviews, then just pick something that appeals to you.

Size and weight

A standard speedcube is about 55 to 57 millimetres wide. If you have small hands, or you're young, a smaller cube (around 54 millimetres) can be more comfortable. Bigger cubes exist too, but feel clumsy to most people. Weight varies more than you'd think: a cube with lots of magnets and a metal core is noticeably heavier.

Setting it up

Most speedcubes can be opened and adjusted. The two most important settings:

  • Tension: how tightly the layers sit against the core. Looser turns lighter and cuts corners better, but a cube that's too loose can pop: a piece flies out. Tighter is more stable but heavier.
  • Compression (on some models): how much the springs or magnets in the axis can still compress. More compression makes the cube more forgiving.

Change one thing at a time, and do a few solves before changing anything else. On many new cubes the factory tension is already reasonable.

Lubricating

A speedcube usually comes lubricated. After a few months it may start to feel dry or scratchy; then a few drops of a silicone-based cube lube help. Don't use kitchen oil or WD-40: they damage the plastic. And don't overdo it: a few drops on the core or between the pieces is enough. A new cube often just needs time to break in, too.

A smart cube

A smart cube has sensors in its core and sends every move over Bluetooth to your computer or phone. With CubeTimer you can connect it in Chrome or Edge: the scramble then lights up move by move, the timer starts on your first turn and stops when the cube is solved. After every solve you see how much time and how many moves the cross, F2L, OLL and PLL took. CubeTimer works with GAN, QiYi, X-Man, MoYu WeiLong AI, Giiker and GoCube.

Smart cubes are more expensive and a bit heavier than an ordinary speedcube from the same brand. As a second cube for analysing your solves they're very useful; as your first cube an ordinary speedcube is the better choice.

Other puzzles

The same goes for the 2x2, 4x4, Pyraminx and Skewb: a magnetic budget version from a known brand is a fine start. On the bigger cubes (5x5 to 7x7) the difference between cheap and expensive is larger, because stability matters more there. Maintenance

  • Don't leave your cube in the sun or in a hot car; the plastic and stickers can warp.
  • If it fell hard on the floor, check that no piece is loose and the tension is still right.
  • Take it apart now and then to remove dust and old lube, for example when it suddenly turns stiffly.

And remember: the best cube is the one you practise with. How to do that most effectively is in how to get faster at cubing.
