Solving the cube

Start with the beginner's method if you can't solve the cube yet. If you can, F2L is the next step, followed by OLL and PLL. Every diagram on this page is drawn from the algorithm underneath it, so what you see always matches what you do.

The beginner's method

You solve the cube layer by layer: the white side first, then the middle layer, then the yellow side. You only need a handful of moves. From step 3 on, always hold the cube with white on the bottom and yellow on top.

How to read the moves

A letter is a quarter turn of that face, clockwise as seen from that side. R is right, L left, U up, D down, F front and B back. An apostrophe means counterclockwise (R') and a 2 means a half turn (R2). That's all there is to it.

Step 1: the white cross

Find the four edges with white on them and place them in a cross around the white centre. Watch the second colour: it has to line up with the centre of that side. So a white and green edge belongs next to the green centre. You do this by feel, without an algorithm; keep trying until it works.

Step 2: the white corners

Turn the cube over so white is on the bottom. Find a corner with white in it, move it above the spot where it belongs, then repeat:

R U R' U'

At most six times and the corner drops into place. If a white corner sits in the bottom layer the wrong way round, do this once to pop it out and start again. These four moves are called the sexy move; you'll see them everywhere.

Step 3: the middle layer

Find an edge in the top layer without yellow. Turn the top so its front colour matches the centre below it, and see which way it needs to go:

to the right: U R U' R' U' F' U F

to the left: U' L' U L U F U' F'

Is an edge in the right spot but flipped? Do either of the two to take it out, then put it back the right way.

Step 4: the yellow cross

Only the top is left. You'll see a dot, an L shape or a line of yellow. These six moves take you one step further each time, until you have a cross:

F R U R' U' F'

Hold an L shape in the top left and a line horizontally. With a dot it doesn't matter; you'll need it three times.

Step 5: orient the yellow corners

Hold a corner that isn't yellow yet at the front right and repeat:

R U R' U R U2 R'

Turn only the top layer to bring the next wrong corner to the front right and do it again. In between the cube looks scrambled; that's expected, it'll come back. This algorithm is called the Sune and is case 27 in OLL.

Step 6: place the corners

The top is yellow, but the corners may be in the wrong places. Find two neighbouring corners that are already right, hold them at the back and do:

U R U' L' U R' U' L

If no pair is right, do it once and look again.

Step 7: place the edges

Three edges left to cycle. Hold the edge that's already right at the back and do:

R U' R U R U R U' R' U' R2

Still not right? Then they cycle the other way: do it twice. This is the Ua perm; you'll find it in PLL as well.

What's next?

That's the whole cube in seven steps. To get faster, replace steps 2 and 3 with F2L (corner and edge together), steps 4 and 5 with OLL (the whole top yellow at once) and steps 6 and 7 with PLL (the top layer solved at once). That's the CFOP method, and it's in the other tabs.

F2L: the first two layers

In the beginner's method you place all the white corners first and then the middle-layer edges. F2L does both at once: you find a corner and the edge that goes next to it, pair them up on top, and insert the pair into its slot in one go. Four pairs, four slots, and your first two layers are done.

The idea matters more than the list. Everything below comes down to three things:

  • Is the pair already joined? Then move it above the right slot with U and insert it with R U R' or F' U' F.
  • Are the corner and edge apart? Bring them together first. That's almost always: open a slot, turn the edge next to the corner, and close the slot again.
  • Is something already in the slot? Take it out with R U R' and treat it as a normal case. That costs a few extra moves, but there's nothing extra to memorise.

OLL: making the top yellow

Your first two layers are done and the top is still a mess. OLL turns it fully yellow with one algorithm. Where the pieces end up doesn't matter yet; PLL sorts that out afterwards.

There are 57 cases. That sounds like a lot, but you recognise them by the shape the yellow makes, and those shapes are grouped below. Start with Cross: there are only seven, and with those seven you can already finish any cube. The rest just saves time.

In the diagram, yellow is what's already facing up and blank is what still needs to be turned. The strips around it are the sides, which show where the yellow is facing now.

PLL: solving the top layer

The top is yellow, but the pieces aren't where they belong yet. PLL puts them in place with one algorithm. Twenty-one cases, and then the cube is solved.

You recognise them by the sides, not the top: two stickers of the same colour next to each other are headlights, three in a row is a bar. That's why the sides are coloured in the diagram, with arrows showing which piece goes where.

Start with the edges only group and the T and J perms: they already cover a large share of your solves, and you can pick up the rest at your own pace.
